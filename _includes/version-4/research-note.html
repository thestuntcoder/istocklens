START WITH A CLAIM

What, exactly,
are you testing?

A useful thesis is specific enough to question. Separate what the business does from what you expect the investment to deliver.

RESEARCH HABIT

Write the claim before looking for reasons to agree with it.

HYPOTHETICAL THESIS / {{ site.data.sample.company }}

“A strong business must be a good investment at today’s price.”

Not established.

The business claim

Is the company financially strong?

The investment claim

Does the price leave room for an acceptable outcome?

Those questions need different evidence. Answering one does not answer the other.

LOOK OUTSIDE THE MODEL

Now challenge
the readout.

Take the important claims back to company filings and sources you trust. Look for what contradicts the story, not just what supports it.

A VALID OUTCOME

“I need more evidence” is a conclusion worth reaching.

01

Check the underlying claim.

Do reported earnings translate into cash? Compare the numbers, periods and definitions in primary documents.

02

Test the assumptions.

What needs to go right to justify the valuation? What happens if growth or margins disappoint?

03

Define the countercase.

What new information would make you reconsider? Record it alongside your thesis.

These are checks for you to perform. This walkthrough does not claim they have been completed.
